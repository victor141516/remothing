# HDR: stream an HDR display as HDR, or convert it to SDR well

- Status: under discussion
- Created: 2026-11-11

## Goal

When the streamed display is in HDR mode, what the client gets today is an
automatic conversion to SDR. Instead:

- a client that supports HDR should get the picture in HDR;
- a client that does not should get a better conversion to SDR than
  today's.

For now this file only records that the work is pending. The user will
take the decisions later; nothing is to be implemented from it yet.

## Open questions

1. **What is wrong with today's SDR picture**, in the user's eyes: washed
   out, too dark, highlights clipped, colours off? That decides what
   "better" means and how to judge a new conversion.
2. **Which half first**: the better SDR conversion (every client gains) or
   HDR to the clients that can show it?
3. **When does a client "support HDR"**: an HDR screen, a decoder for
   10-bit video, and a browser that can put HDR on a canvas are three
   different things. All three, detected automatically, or a switch in the
   client?
4. **HDR in which layer**: only the video layer, or the lossless layer too?
   Lossless tiles are 8-bit today (see below); pixel-exact HDR tiles are
   bigger and a larger change.
5. **Clients of both kinds on one display**: two pictures of the same
   display (HDR and SDR) at once, or one for all?
6. **Settings**: is the SDR conversion fixed, or adjustable (brightness of
   SDR white, how highlights roll off)?

## What happens today

Read from the code; not observed on an HDR display.

- Windows capture asks Desktop Duplication for 8-bit BGRA and nothing else:
  `DuplicateOutput1(device, 0, &[DXGI_FORMAT_B8G8R8A8_UNORM])` in
  `duplicate`, `crates/platform-windows/src/dxgi.rs`. On an HDR desktop
  (16-bit float) Windows converts the image to SDR before we see it. So
  the conversion is not ours: there is nothing in it to tune.
- On systems where only the older `DuplicateOutput` works, an HDR desktop
  is not captured at all: the frame is rejected with "desktop image format
  ... is not BGRA" (same file).
- Everything after capture is 8-bit SDR: the shared screen copy and the
  tile comparison (BGRX), the lossless tiles, the encoder input texture
  (`DXGI_FORMAT_B8G8R8A8_UNORM`), the video stream (marked BT.709, limited
  range, in `crates/encoder/src/ffmpeg.rs`), the client's textures and its
  canvas.
- Linux: X11 and Xvfb have no HDR, so this is a Windows matter for now.

## What it would touch

A sketch for whoever picks this up, not a design.

Capture, for both halves:
- Ask duplication for the 16-bit float format, and find out whether the
  display is in HDR and how bright it goes (`IDXGIOutput6::GetDesc1`).
- HDR can be switched on and off while streaming: the capture has to
  reopen, as it does for a mode change.
- The tile comparison (CPU and GPU) works on 8-bit BGRA today.

Better SDR:
- Our own conversion in a shader, before the comparison: from the float
  image to 8-bit, with the display's SDR white level and peak brightness
  as inputs. Everything after it stays as it is.

HDR to the client:
- Video in 10 bits (HEVC Main 10 or AV1), signalled as BT.2020 with the PQ
  curve, with the display's HDR metadata. The codec is already negotiated
  per client (`56fe10a`); 10-bit profiles would join that negotiation.
  H.264 is not an option for this.
- The lossless layer: 8-bit tiles cannot carry HDR (open question 4).
- The client: detect the screen (`matchMedia('(dynamic-range: high)')`)
  and the decoder (`VideoDecoder.isConfigSupported` with a 10-bit codec
  string), and draw on a canvas that shows HDR. Our picture goes through
  WebGL2; whether a WebGL canvas can show HDR in the browsers we use has
  to be checked first, because it may be what decides the design.

From the Sunshine study (task 003), which does both: it asks duplication
for 16-bit float first, has one set of shaders for PQ output and another
for linear to sRGB, sends HDR only when the client asks for it and the
display is in HDR, and needs HEVC or AV1 in 10 bits for it
(`src/platform/windows/display_vram.cpp:~2025-2046`,
`src/platform/windows/display_base.cpp:~772-840`,
`src/video_colorspace.cpp:49-105` in `.tools/ref/Sunshine`). It avoids the
10-bit integer capture format because it "seems to actually just clamp"
the float values.

## Testing

Needs a display in HDR mode. Switching HDR on the user's live display
changes what they see: ask first (`AGENTS.md`).

## Not verified

- How Windows' own conversion looks next to any alternative: nothing was
  captured or compared.
- HDR on a WebGL canvas, and 10-bit decoding in low-latency mode, in any
  browser.
- That the NVENC, AMF and QuickSync paths we use through FFmpeg accept
  10-bit input from a D3D11 texture.
